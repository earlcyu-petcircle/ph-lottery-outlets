PH LOTTO OUTLETS

Are Philippine lotto outlets concentrated in poorer or richer neighborhoods?

Lotto outlets follow commerce, not poverty

On the open data, they are concentrated in richer ones, not poorer ones. Richer places have more and closer outlets nationally, but that gap mostly reflects where commerce is, and where everyone lives among shops, as in Metro Manila, it disappears.

Earl YuNovember 2026Open and published data onlyRead the full paper

WHY ASK /

Is the lottery a tax on the poor here too?

The lottery is often dubbed as "a tax on the poor" since poorer households spend a far bigger share of their income on lotto tickets than richer ones; Johnny Harris's video The US Lottery is a Lie makes that case for the US.

There are two ways to test this: access and spend. This study focuses on access: are Philippine lotto outlets concentrated in poorer or richer neighbourhoods? Spend, whether poorer households spend more on tickets, needs household budget data and is out of scope.

The Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office (PCSO) runs the country's lotteries. It recorded a revenue of PHP 62.35 billion in 2024 and operates 7,443 outlets, more than the number of 7-Eleven stores (about 4,000) and Jollibee branches (about 1,300) in the country.

THREE KEY INSIGHTS /

What the data say

Wealth is Meta's Relative Wealth Index (RWI). Each wealth quintile holds the same number of people, from Q1, the poorest, to Q5, the richest.

01 / THE COUNTRY

Nationally, richer places have more outlets per person, and closer ones

31xmore outlets per 100,000 people in the wealthiest quintile (12.7) than the poorest one (0.4)
11.5 vs 0.6 kmmedian road distance to the nearest outlet, poorest against richest wealth quintile
2% vs 92%of people within 2 km of an outlet by road, poorest against richest wealth quintile

On access, the national picture is the reverse of "a tax on the poor": the poorest Filipinos have the fewest outlets and the furthest to go. 99.5% of people in the poorest wealth quintile live in a tile with no outlet, against 5.5% in the richest.

02 / THE REASON

Outlets follow commerce, and that explains most of the rich-poor gap

0.19 vs 5.74shops per 1,000 people, poorest against richest wealth quintile; the urban share rises from 38% to 99.8%
2.45 to 1.17the wealth rate ratio, with wealth only and then with urban share and shops held fixed
1.16 to 1.18the ratio with shops through the spatial refits and outlet sensitivities

A lotto terminal is a counter in a shop, a mall or a market, and in the Philippines shops cluster in towns, which are richer than the countryside around them. Most of the national gap is where shops and towns are: with urban share and shops held fixed, the wealth rate ratio falls from 2.45 to 1.17, a small lean towards richer places that holds through the spatial refits and outlet sensitivities (1.16 to 1.18). The ratio is how many times as many outlets per person a place one standard deviation richer has (0.49 RWI points); 1 means no difference.

03 / THE CITIES

In Metro Manila, wealth makes no difference

12.5 to 15.2outlets per 100,000 people in every Metro Manila wealth quintile
99%of Metro Manila's people live within 2 km of the nearest lotto outlet
0.4 to 0.5 kmmedian road distance in every Metro Manila wealth quintile

However, Metro Manila paints a different picture: an outlet within about 400 m for everyone, whatever their wealth. Almost everyone lives in urban cells and every wealth quintile has 5.1 to 7.5 shops per 1,000 people, so outlets are close to everyone. Metro Davao repeats the national gradient (12.5 km by road in its poorest wealth quintile against 0.7 km in its richest), and Metro Cebu sits in between.

DATA AND METHODOLOGY /

How it was measured

The full design, every model and every check are in the paper.

  • Count outlets where wealth is measured. The main unit is the Relative Wealth Index's own 2.4 km tile (40,737 with people). Each barangay's 2024 census count is spread over the 100 m pixels where its people live. Barangays (41,923) are the check.
  • Compare per person. Outlets per 100,000 people by wealth quintile, then negative binomial count models with population as the offset and province intercepts.
  • Add commerce step by step. Wealth only, then urban share, shops (OpenStreetMap plus Overture) and road length, with spatial refits and outlet sensitivities as checks.
  • Zoom into three cities. The same models inside Metro Manila, Metro Cebu and Metro Davao by barangay, with errors clustered by tile.
  • Measure distance by road. From every 100 m pixel where people live to the nearest outlet over the OpenStreetMap road network.

LIMITATIONS /

What this can't say, and what to hold loosely

Each is set out in full in the paper's limitations.

What it can't say

  • Access is not spending. Fewer outlets near poorer people does not mean poorer people buy fewer tickets: they may buy where they work, shop or travel.
  • Nothing here measures share of income. A PHP 20 play is a larger share of a small income wherever it is bought, so the spending claim stays open and needs household budget data.
  • Association, not cause. The ratios describe where outlets are relative to wealth, shops and towns; they do not say why PCSO and its agents put them there.
  • Lotto only. The Small Town Lottery brought in PHP 31.76 billion of PCSO's PHP 62.35 billion gaming revenue in 2024, and its booths are not published.

Outlets follow commerce, not poverty, so if the lottery is a tax on the poor in the Philippines, the case has to be made on spending, not on access.

What to hold loosely

  • The wealth index's own error is not carried through. Its per-tile error (mean 0.53) is large next to Metro Manila's barangay spread of 0.20, so intervals are too narrow and a flat result like Manila's is weaker evidence than it looks.
  • Shop coverage is uneven. 6.1 shops per 1,000 people in Metro Manila against 0.2 in BARMM, so the with-shops ratios rest on a control that is itself tied to wealth.
  • PCSO's list is undated. 7,443 outlets against 6,494 agents at the end of 2023, so it may hold closed outlets; dropping or adding doubtful ones moves the national ratios by 0.02 at most.
  • 2.4 km tiles are coarse inside cities, and the layers come from different years: wealth from 2021, people from 2024, outlets, shops and roads from 2026.
  • BARMM is thin: 15 outlets for 5.7 million people and the weakest wealth coverage.
