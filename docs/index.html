PH / LOTTO OUTLETS / 2026

Is the lottery a tax on the poor, or just on the busy street?

Are the Philippine Charity Sweepstakes Office's (PCSO) lotto outlets concentrated in poorer neighbourhoods? On the open data, no. Per person, outlets are about 30 times as common in the richest fifth of the country as in the poorest. Most of that gap goes with where shops and towns are, and among places with the same amount of retail, richer places still have about 1.2 times as many outlets per person. Outlets follow commerce and urbanisation, not poverty. That says nothing about who buys tickets or what share of their income they spend, and every result here is an association, not a cause.

WHY ASK /

Two claims in one charge

The question comes from Johnny Harris's video The US Lottery is a Lie, which makes the case that lottery revenue leans on lower-income players: the familiar charge that the lottery is "a tax on the poor". This study asks whether the same story can be seen on a map of the Philippines.

The charge bundles two claims. The access claim is that outlets are concentrated in poorer neighbourhoods, which a map can test. The spending claim is that poorer households spend a larger share of income on tickets, which only household budget data can test. This study tests the access claim.

FINDINGS /

Four questions, four answers

Wealth is Meta's Relative Wealth Index, and a model's wealth ratio is how many times as many outlets per person a place one standard deviation richer has (0.49 RWI points, roughly the step between neighbouring wealth fifths in the middle of the country). Above 1 means more outlets in richer places.

01 / SUPPLY PER PERSON

Richer places have far more outlets per person

After accounting for population, are outlets more or less common per person in poorer neighbourhoods?

Outlets per person climb with every step up in wealth. Splitting the country's people into five equal groups by the wealth of their tile, the poorest fifth has 0.4 outlets per 100,000 people and the richest 12.7, about 30 times as many. Almost everyone in the poorest fifth (99.5%) lives in a tile with no outlet at all, against 5.5% of the richest fifth.

The count model, which also holds province and urban share fixed, puts the gap at 2.13 times as many outlets per person for a tile one standard deviation richer (95% CI 1.96-2.32; 2.36 on barangays). The presence model says the same about having any outlet at all: an odds ratio of 2.18 (1.92-2.46).

02 / COMMERCE

Most of the gap goes with shops, and what is left still leans richer

Does any wealth gradient hold among places with the same amount of shopping?

The poorest fifth of tiles has 0.19 shops per 1,000 people, and 38% of its people live in urban cells; the richest fifth has 5.7 shops per 1,000, and 99.8% of its people are urban. Adding shops to the model shrinks the wealth ratio from 2.13 to 1.17, and adding road length as well leaves it at 1.24.

So among tiles in the same province with the same shops and urban share, a tile one standard deviation richer still has about 1.2 times as many outlets per person: a small lean towards richer places, not poorer ones. It barely moves when the model allows for spatial clustering (1.18), drops GPS points that disagree with their address (1.16) or adds outlets placed only at a town centre (1.17). Whether shops are a confounder or the route by which wealth and outlets meet cannot be told apart here, so both readings stand.

03 / WITHIN CITIES

Inside the cities the pattern splits

Does the gradient hold inside Metro Manila, Metro Cebu and Metro Davao, where rich and poor neighbourhoods sit side by side?

Metro Manila is flat. Every count variant sits between about 0.9 and 1.0 and every interval includes 1. Every wealth fifth of its barangays has between 12.5 and 15.2 outlets per 100,000 people, and the poorest fifth is, if anything, the highest.

Metro Davao leans rich in every variant: 4.55 with urban share, 3.47 with shops, and 1.97 with shops and roads, all with intervals above 1. Its rate climbs from 0.1 per 100,000 in the poorest fifth of barangays (one outlet among about 687,000 people) to 12.7 in the richest.

Metro Cebu is unclear once shops are controlled. Its richer barangays have more outlets per person with urban share alone (2.34), but with shops the ratio is 1.23 (0.65-2.33): among barangays with the same retail, richer and poorer ones cannot be told apart.

04 / DISTANCE

The poorest live furthest from an outlet, except in Metro Manila

How far do people in poorer and richer neighbourhoods live from their nearest outlet, by road?

The median person in the poorest fifth of tiles lives 11.5 km from an outlet by road, only 2% live within 2 km, and 4.4% have no road route to an outlet at all. In the richest fifth the median is 0.6 km and 92% live within 2 km.

Metro Manila is the opposite case: the median is 0.4 to 0.5 km in every wealth fifth, and 99% of people live within 2 km of an outlet. Metro Davao repeats the national ladder (12.5 km down to 0.7 km), and in Metro Cebu only the poorest fifth stands apart (4.5 km, then 1.0 km or less).

WHAT IT MEANS /

Not an access story

The access version of "a tax on the poor" expects more outlets per person where people are poorer. PCSO's lotto network shows the reverse nationally, and no lean towards the poor inside any of the three cities. What outlets go with is commerce and urbanisation. A lotto terminal is a counter in a shop, a mall or a market, so outlets sit where shops sit, and in the Philippines shops sit in towns and cities, which are richer than the countryside around them. A plausible reading is that outlets are placed for foot traffic rather than aimed at poor neighbourhoods; the data are consistent with that, but cannot test it.

Metro Manila is urban throughout and its barangays span a narrow band of wealth, so there is no gradient to find. Metro Davao, drawn by law to take in Davao City's uplands, the southern municipalities and the Island Garden City of Samal as well as its urban core, repeats the national contrast between town and hinterland inside one boundary. Metro Cebu sits between the two.

The result sits with the lottery side of earlier studies, not the gaming-machine side: gaming machines and betting shops have been found to follow deprivation, while in New Jersey and Massachusetts lottery retail did not. It adds a middle-income Asian case to a set of studies that had none.

What this cannot say

  • Access is not spending. Fewer outlets near poorer people does not mean poorer people buy fewer tickets: they may buy where they work, shop or travel.
  • Nothing here measures share of income. A PHP 20 play is a larger share of a small income wherever it is bought, so the spending claim stays open and needs household budget data.
  • Association, not cause. The ratios describe where outlets are relative to wealth, shops and towns; they do not say why PCSO and its agents put them there.
  • Lotto only. The Small Town Lottery brought in PHP 31.76 billion of PCSO's PHP 62.35 billion gaming revenue in 2024, and its booths are not published.

If the lottery is a tax on the poor in the Philippines, the case has to be made on spending, not on access.

METHOD /

How it was measured

  • Count outlets where wealth is measured. The main unit is the Relative Wealth Index's own 2.4 km tile (40,737 with people). Each barangay's 2024 census count is spread over the 100 m WorldPop pixels where its people live and summed per tile. Barangays (41,923) are the check.
  • Compare per person. A negative binomial count model with population as the offset, so results read as outlets per person, and province intercepts to absorb regional differences and PCSO branch practice.
  • Add commerce step by step. Each model is shown with wealth only, with urban share, with shops (OpenStreetMap plus Overture) and with road length, because commerce may be a mediator as much as a confounder.
  • Check it several ways. A presence model for having any outlet, spatial refits where residuals cluster, and sensitivities that drop doubtful GPS points or add outlets placed only at a town centre.
  • Zoom into three cities. The same models inside Metro Manila, Metro Cebu and Metro Davao by barangay, with city and municipality intercepts and errors clustered by tile. Each city's boundary comes from an official source.
  • Measure distance by road. From every 100 m pixel where people live to the nearest outlet over the OpenStreetMap road network.

LIMITATIONS /

What to hold loosely

Each of these is set out in full in the paper's limitations section.

  • The wealth index's own error is not carried through. Its per-tile error (mean 0.53) is large next to Metro Manila's barangay spread of 0.20, so intervals are too narrow, most of all inside cities, and a flat result like Manila's is weaker evidence than it looks.
  • Shop coverage is uneven. OpenStreetMap and Overture list 6.1 shops per 1,000 people in Metro Manila against 0.2 in BARMM, so the with-shops ratios rest on a control that is itself tied to wealth.
  • PCSO's list is undated. It lists 7,443 outlets against 6,494 agents at the end of 2023, so it may hold closed outlets. 26 could not be placed, 133 only at a town centre; dropping or adding these moves the national ratios by 0.02 at most.
  • 2.4 km tiles are coarse inside cities. Metro Manila's 1,711 barangays have most of their people in 113 tiles, so neighbouring barangays often share one wealth value.
  • The layers come from different years: wealth from 2021, people from 2024, outlets, shops and roads from 2026.
  • Urban share cannot tell a town centre from a suburb, and BARMM is thin: 15 outlets for 5.7 million people and the weakest wealth coverage.

Run it yourself

Needs Python 3.12, curl and tippecanoe, about 1.2 GB of downloads and 5 GB of memory. The 2024 census workbooks are fetched by hand first, as data-quality.md describes. On the same downloads a run reproduces the committed tables byte for byte.

The code, five narrative notebooks and the full paper are in the GitHub repository. Code is MIT licensed. The wealth values come from the RWI, which is CC BY-NC 4.0, so anything built on them is for non-commercial use with attribution.

git clone https://github.com/earlcyu-petcircle/ph-lottery-outlets.git
cd ph-lottery-outlets
./run_all.sh            # collect, build, analyse, map
python3 src/04_build_map/serve_map.py
# this page at http://localhost:8000/
